Kundli Milan: beyond the 36 gunas

The guna score is the first thing families ask about and the last thing a careful astrologer relies on alone. Here is what the eight kootas measure, and what they leave out.

By Chandan Shrivastava · 7 min read · 9 November 2026

Ask most families what the astrologer said about a proposed match and you will hear a number: “27 gunas”, “only 16”, “32 out of 36”. The Ashtakoot score is useful, but it is the start of a matching, not the end of one.

The eight kootas

Ashtakoot Milan compares the two birth Moons, their signs and their Nakshatras, across eight categories called kootas. Each koota carries a maximum number of points, and the total is 36.

KootaWhat it comparesPoints
VarnaTemperament and spiritual outlook, from the Moon sign1
VashyaMutual attraction and who tends to lead2
TaraHarmony between the two birth Nakshatras3
YoniPhysical and instinctive compatibility4
Graha MaitriFriendship between the lords of the two Moon signs5
GanaBasic nature: Deva, Manushya or Rakshasa6
BhakootRelative placement of the Moon signs: family, finance, wellbeing7
NadiConstitution, health and progeny8
Total36

Notice the weighting. The first four kootas together carry only 10 points. The last four, Graha Maitri, Gana, Bhakoot and Nadi, carry 26. A low score in Nadi or Bhakoot matters much more than a low score in Varna.

Why 18 is the usual threshold

Eighteen is half of 36, and tradition treats it as the minimum for an acceptable match. Scores of 18 to 24 are usually called average, 25 to 32 good, and above 32 excellent. These bands are convenient, but they are conventions. They describe how well two Moons sit together. They say nothing about the rest of either chart.

When a high score is still a poor match

The whole Ashtakoot system is built on the Moon alone. Two people can score 30 or more and still face real difficulty if, for example:

  • one chart has a badly afflicted 7th house or 7th lord;
  • Venus, the significator of marriage, is weak or heavily afflicted;
  • the Navamsa shows a strained marriage picture;
  • both partners enter difficult Dashas at the same time soon after the wedding.

None of these appear in the guna score.

When a low score can still be fine

The reverse is just as common. A couple might score 16 because of a Bhakoot or Nadi mismatch, yet the doshas behind those points may be cancelled, and the 7th houses, Venus and Navamsa of both charts may be strong and supportive. Rejecting such a match on the number alone can mean turning down a very good marriage.

Dosha cancellations

The two most feared mismatches are Nadi dosha (both partners in the same Nadi, which scores 0 out of 8) and Bhakoot dosha (certain unfavourable placements of the two Moon signs, which score 0 out of 7). Classical texts list exceptions under which these doshas are considered cancelled. For Nadi dosha, the commonly cited exceptions include:

  • both Moons in the same rashi but in different Nakshatras;
  • both Moons in the same Nakshatra but in different rashis;
  • the same Nakshatra but different padas, according to some authorities.

For Bhakoot dosha, texts mention cancellation when the lords of the two Moon signs are the same planet or are natural friends, among other conditions. Different traditions weigh these exceptions differently, so a careful astrologer states which rule is being applied and why, rather than simply adding or subtracting points.

What else a careful astrologer checks

A proper matching reads both charts in full, alongside the guna score:

  • The 7th house and its lord in each chart: their sign, strength, aspects and the planets placed there.
  • Venus for both, and Jupiter as well, which traditionally represents the husband in a woman’s chart.
  • The Navamsa (D9), the divisional chart most closely tied to marriage and to the underlying strength of each planet.
  • Mangal dosha in both charts. Mars in certain houses is checked for each partner, along with its classical cancellations. When both partners carry it, it is generally considered balanced.
  • Running Dashas. The planetary periods each person will be in during the early years of marriage, and whether they support each other.
  • General health indicators, discussed responsibly and never as a prediction.

Putting the number in its place

The Ashtakoot score is a quick, standardised first filter, and it is worth knowing. But it compares two Moons, not two lives. Treat 18 as a guide, look for cancellations before declaring a dosha, and make sure someone has read both full charts before a decision is taken.

It also helps to be honest about what any matching can do. Astrology can show where two charts support each other and where friction is likely: different rhythms, different expectations around money or family, periods when one partner will be under more strain. That is useful knowledge for a couple and their families. What it cannot do is replace the ordinary work of getting to know each other, talking openly about values and plans, and listening to what the families observe. A good astrologer’s opinion should sit alongside those things, not above them.

Finally, accurate birth details matter. The Moon’s Nakshatra can change within a day, and a wrong birth date or a guessed time can move a Moon across a Nakshatra or even a sign boundary, changing several koota scores at once. Before reading too much into any result, check that both sets of birth data are as reliable as they can be.
