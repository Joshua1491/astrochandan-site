Why your Vedic sign is different

If your Vedic chart calls you a Cancer when you have always been a Leo, nobody has made a mistake. The two systems start the zodiac in different places.

By Chandan Shrivastava · 6 min read · 14 November 2026

It is a common surprise. You have read horoscopes as a Leo for years, then an astrologer in India casts your chart and calls you a Cancer. Nothing is wrong with either chart. The two systems simply measure the zodiac from different starting points.

Two zodiacs

Western astrology mostly uses the tropical zodiac. It begins the zodiac at the March equinox, the moment the Sun crosses the celestial equator heading north, and calls that point 0° Aries regardless of which stars lie behind it. The tropical zodiac is tied to the seasons.

Vedic astrology, Jyotish, uses the sidereal zodiac. It measures the signs against the fixed stars themselves. In the Lahiri system, 0° Aries is fixed so that the star Spica (Chitra) sits at exactly 0° Libra, on the opposite side of the zodiac.

About 1,700 years ago the two zodiacs were aligned. Since then they have drifted apart.

Precession: why they drift

The Earth’s axis wobbles very slowly, like a spinning top, completing one circle in roughly 25,800 years. This is called the precession of the equinoxes. As a result, the point of the March equinox slides backwards against the background stars by about one degree every 72 years.

The tropical zodiac moves with the equinox. The sidereal zodiac stays with the stars. The gap between them grows by a little under a minute of arc each year.

The ayanamsa

That gap has a name in Jyotish: the ayanamsa. It is the number of degrees you subtract from a tropical position to get the sidereal one. Today it is about 24°. Several ayanamsas exist because astronomers and astrologers have proposed slightly different starting points, but the one most widely used in India is the Lahiri ayanamsa, also called Chitrapaksha, which was adopted for the Indian national calendar. In late 2026 the Lahiri value is roughly 24°13′.

All charts on this site, and in Chandan’s consultations, use the sidereal zodiac with Lahiri ayanamsa.

Why your sign usually moves back one

Each sign is 30° wide. Subtracting about 24° moves most positions back into the previous sign. Only if a planet sits in the last six degrees or so of a tropical sign does it stay in the same sign after the correction.

So for roughly four people in five, the Vedic Sun sign is one sign earlier than the Western one. A tropical Leo born in early or mid-August becomes a sidereal Cancer. A tropical Leo born in the last few days of the Leo period, around 17 to 22 August, usually stays a Leo. The same logic applies to the Moon, the ascendant and every planet.

Neither result is “wrong”. They are two reference frames. But if you are using Vedic techniques such as Nakshatras, Dashas or Ashtakoot matching, you need the sidereal positions, because those techniques were built on them.

The same correction applies to your whole chart, not just the Sun. Your Moon, ascendant and every planet move back by the same amount. That is why a Western natal chart and a Vedic one, cast for the same moment, look so different at first glance, even though the planets are in exactly the same places in the sky. The houses can change too, since Vedic astrology traditionally uses whole signs as houses, counting from the sign that holds the ascendant.

Why Jyotish cares more about the Moon and Lagna

In Western popular astrology, the Sun sign is the headline. Jyotish looks first at two other points:

  • The Moon sign (Rashi). The Moon represents the mind, emotions and daily experience. Your Moon sign is the basis for Vedic horoscopes, for Sade Sati and other transit judgements, and for marriage matching. Your birth Nakshatra, the lunar mansion the Moon occupied at birth, also sets the starting point of your Vimshottari Dasha.
  • The Lagna (ascendant). The sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth. It fixes the twelve houses of the chart, and so decides which areas of life each planet influences. The Lagna changes roughly every two hours, which is why an accurate birth time matters.

The Sun still matters, especially for vitality, the father, authority and career. But when a Vedic astrologer says “your sign”, they usually mean your Moon sign.

How to find yours

You need your date of birth, place of birth and, ideally, an accurate time of birth.

  • For your Moon sign and birth Nakshatra, use the free Moon sign calculator. The Moon moves quickly, roughly one sign every two and a quarter days, so even an approximate time usually gives the right sign.
  • For your Lagna, planet positions, Navamsa and Dasha, use the free Janam Kundli. Here the birth time needs to be as precise as you can make it.

If the results surprise you, that is normal. Read the description for your sidereal Moon sign rather than your Western Sun sign, and see whether it fits better. Many people find that it does.
